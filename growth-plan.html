Growth Plan — AI Project Launchpad

NxtWave Growth Intern Challenge · Round 1

Goal 500 registrations
Budget ₹2,000 · Window 7 days
Audience Final-year engineering students

Plan to get 500 final-year students into a free 60-minute AI workshop — without spending a rupee on ads.

The plan is not a channel list; it is one loop. The product converts a visitor into a project-matched registrant and hands them a referral link at the moment of peak enthusiasm. Peer sharing then carries half the target at zero cost per acquisition, and the ₹2,000 buys the permission and creative that start the loop.

1 — Who exactly, and why they care

Primary persona — “Placement Season Priya”

  • Final year (2026), tier-2/3 engineering college, CSE/IT/AI-DS, 6.5–8 CGPA
  • Knows basic Python; has 2–3 academic projects nobody would ask about
  • Placements start in ~6 weeks; resume has no project she is proud of
  • Phone-first. Gets information from WhatsApp groups and seniors, not LinkedIn

Why she registers (motivation ladder)

  • Surface: “AI is the future.” Gets a click, not attendance
  • Utility: AI appears in job descriptions she is applying to
  • Identity: wants to be someone who builds AI, not someone who watches it
  • Fear: final year ends in months and the resume is undefendable — the real driver

2 — The five registration triggers, in the order they fire

1. A specific idea, not a topic

“AI Resume Analyzer” beats “learn AI”. The hero generator removes the “I don’t know what I’d build” objection in two seconds.

2. A 60-minute single sitting

Small enough to say yes to immediately; the deadline is why they actually finish. Every catalogue project is scoped to it.

3. Zero prerequisites, stated

Not just “beginners welcome” — we name what they need (a laptop) and what they don’t (GPU, paid tools, prior AI).

4. Visible peers already in

Seat counter, activity ticker and leaderboard make it look like something their classmates joined.

5. A reason to invite friends

Referral code + milestone ladder turns registration into an entry ticket to a game, not a form submit.

Explicitly rejected

Paid ads (wrong audience at ₹2,000), cash prizes (unaffordable, non-compounding), fake testimonials and fake expiry timers (trust-destroying).

The model is falsifiable: if visitor→generator drops below 65%, the hero is wrong and we fix copy before spending. If the referral curve flattens on day 3, the loop is broken — not the traffic.

The loop that makes 500 possible

Register→ Get a matched project→ Get a referral code→ Share on WhatsApp→ Friends register→ Rank rises→ Share again
  • Share at peak enthusiasm: the ask lands seconds after they receive their own project idea
  • Ladder starts at 1 referral — instantly earnable; runs to 10 (Builder Wall feature)
  • Status, not cash: a public rank keeps students sharing for 7 days; a ₹100 voucher would not
  • ₹0 marginal cost: every reward is digital — templates, priority Q&A, a place on the wall

Why no paid ads

At ₹2,000, Meta ads buy roughly ₹40 CPM of attention from people who are not final-year students. The same money pays 10 ambassadors who already sit inside the right WhatsApp groups and cost nothing to trust.

7 — Metrics, experiments, and the judgment calls behind them

Metrics

  • North star: 500 verified registrations, 60%+ attendance
  • Leading: visitor→generator ≥65%, generator→reg start ≥75%, sharing rate ≥20%
  • Guardrails: <₹15 CPR per channel, no college >15% of total, one referral credit per verified email

Experiments (2 already live in the build)

  • Hero A/B (live): “Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes” vs “Stop Watching AI Tutorials. Build One.” — metric: landing→registration_started
  • CTA A/B (live): “Reserve My Free Spot” vs “Build My First AI Project” — metric: CTA CTR
  • Referral ask: 3-friend vs 5-friend milestone — metric: share→signup
  • Ambassador framing: “share with friends” vs “get 20 from your college”

If it breaks, where

  • Groups ignore the link → lead with a project idea, not an invitation
  • Form drop-off → registrants arrive pre-primed from the generator; 6 fields, no payment, no OTP
  • Sharing stops by day 2 → public daily re-ranking, and milestone 1 lands after a single referral

8 — What AI suggested that I rejected (and why)

“Fake 24-hour scarcity timers”

Rejected. A timer that resets daily destroys trust the moment a student notices. The 500-seat cap is real scarcity — use that instead.

“Testimonials from past students”

Rejected. The workshop has not run yet. Fabricated quotes are the fastest way to make a reviewer discount the whole submission, so the page states expected outcomes instead.

“12-channel + LLM-chatbot plan”

Rejected. Breadth is not a strategy in 7 days with ₹2,000. Cut to 5 ranked channels with budgeted yields, and made the recommender rule-based so the demo never depends on an API key.
