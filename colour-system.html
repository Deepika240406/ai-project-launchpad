1 — The one rule that fixes everything

Indigo leads. Lime highlights. Nothing else competes.

#CCFF4D scores 1.17:1 on white and 1.02:1 on the progress track — it cannot be read, outlined, or seen as a dot. But put ink on top of it and it becomes the strongest pair in the system at 16.39:1. That is a highlighter, and a highlighter is all it is.

So the chrome went to indigo (#4F46E5, 6.29:1 — works as text AND under white text), and lime was reduced to four deliberate appearances: the headline swash, the registration success moment, the one card theme students can pick, and a fold whisper. One accent doing one job loudly beats three accents all doing half a job.

3 — Why the chart has these six colours

Two earlier versions failed. #5B3DF5 next to #7C5CF5 were two violets nobody can tell apart in a stacked bar. Then a light grey-blue for the smallest channel — quiet-looking, but at 2.56:1 it was not a visible mark. On a white chart every mark must be mid-to-dark, which collapses the lightness range, so hue is the only separator left. These six sit 45°–179° apart and all clear 3:1 on white.

WCAG 1.4.11 applies to graphics required to understand content. A separator between two sections is not one — darkening every hairline would make the page noisy for no accessibility gain. Those three exemptions are pinned in the test file with the reason written next to them, so they are decisions rather than oversights.
